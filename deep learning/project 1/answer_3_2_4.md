When retraining the Iris model with mean_subtraction=False and normalization=False, the final accuracy dropped slightly from 98.33% to 97.50%, though it still exceeded the 90% requirement. More notably, the training became less stable with greater fluctuations in accuracy (e.g., dropping to 88.33% at epoch 76) compared to the smooth convergence when preprocessing was applied. Without mean subtraction and normalization, features with different scales (sepal length ~5-7cm vs. petal width ~0-2cm) cause the gradient updates to be dominated by larger-magnitude features, leading to slower and less stable convergence. Preprocessing helps center the data and scale features uniformly, allowing the tanh activation functions to operate in their sensitive region and enabling more consistent gradient flow.
